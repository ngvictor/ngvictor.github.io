A product vision for running real work events in VR. I designed the audience and presenter experiences, including the 2D tooling a host uses to set up and run the whole event.

Background

How this came about

This started with a pivot. I was designing Workplace, Meta's enterprise intranet, when the business moved into the metaverse and our team's roadmap moved with it.

Nobody on the team had designed for VR before, me included. I taught myself to design and prototype in the medium, then ran prototyping sessions to upskill the rest of the team as they made the same jump.

The design work started with research rather than screens. I mapped the presenter's journey and the audience's journey end to end, from the calendar invite to the conversations afterward, and those maps decided where VR adds something real and where 2D should stay.

The work was presented and demoed live to Andrew Bosworth, Meta's CTO, and senior VPs across Reality Labs.

In 2D

The 2D redesign

Every VR event still starts on a screen. I redesigned both sides of it: how you attend, and how you create and produce.

Attending

Before

Entry points were scattered across surfaces, and the audience had no home before the event.

After

One go-to page: watch in 2D or join in VR, questions and polls in one place, and the recording afterward.

Creating

Before

Live and Events sat in separate tools, entry points were hidden, and there was no central place for insights.

After

A central surface for 2D, VR or hybrid events, with delegation, collaboration and insights built in.

Producing

Before

Going live was overwhelming, with no guidance and no support in the moment.

After

A guided setup, moderators and producers alongside, and full control of layouts, branding and scenes.

Event Studio: plan the whole event from 2D surfaces organizers already know. Big events take several people to plan, and none of that should happen in a headset.
